Hex Truchet Loops

Each hexagon takes one of two mirror tiles. Click one to flip it.

How it works

A tile joins its six edges in three pairs, drawn as a corridor through the hexagon. Corridors always meet across shared edges, so the figure is a set of closed loops, and each one gets its own colour. Flipping a tile changes the loop count by −2 if its three corridors lie on three different loops (they merge), 0 if on two (they rearrange), or +2 if on one (it splits) — never an odd number, which is why a single loop is hard to reach: you can never merge just two.

Colour follows the loop, not its size, so anything a flip does not touch keeps its colour. Rhombi fills each loop's territory instead of drawing its curve: a tile cuts its hexagon into three rhombi, one per corner it wraps, and each belongs to exactly one loop. Show outer path brings back whatever runs out past the tiles, which is hidden by default; the count is always of the paths you can see. Lock border pins the outer ring so its walls close into a band, turning the figure into an enclosed maze; with it on, an odd radius can be clicked down to a single enclosed corridor, while an even one bottoms out at two.

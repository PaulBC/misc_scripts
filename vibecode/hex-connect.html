Hex Connect

Click a hexagon to flip its tile. Join every curve into one loop, in as few flips as you can.

5
How it works

A tile joins its six edges in three pairs, so the curves always meet across shared edges and the board is a set of closed loops, each in its own color. Flipping a tile changes the loop count by −2 if its three curves lie on three different loops (they merge), 0 if on two (they swap territory), or +2 if on one (it splits). It is never an odd number, so you can never merge just two loops, and a board with n loops needs at least (n−1)/2 flips.

Par is the exact fewest flips from the board you were dealt. There are 1,848,868 single-loop tilings of this board, and par is the distance to the nearest one. Merging whenever you can is usually right but not always: sometimes the only way to par is a flip that leaves the count alone, and taking the obvious merge costs you two flips later.

Hint rings a cell on some shortest path from where you are now, preferring one that merges loops and never one that splits. Every flip moves you exactly one flip closer to a single loop, or one further.

On a first visit the page builds the table of every solution, which takes about ten seconds, and keeps it in the browser for next time.
